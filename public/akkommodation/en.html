01 / The object

Four layers.

A white shell. A metal dome. A hollow inside it. And in front, a lens. Only the dome moves.

ShellDomeHollowLens

01 / Shell

Concentric steps lead inward, like terraces into a funnel. Left and right they open into the corners of an eye. There are no lids. The eye is always open.

02 / Dome

Brushed, satin, about 23 centimetres across. No painted iris. It turns toward you when you move.

03 / Hollow

A soft recess at the centre. Its darkness comes from depth, not paint. Fine pale veins run outward from it.

04 / Lens

Thin, clear, curved. Fixed in the innermost step, it never moves. Fine circuit traces sit on its inner side. The eye always looks through it.

60Centimetres wide

02Axes of movement

01Lens that holds still

Renders show the intended finish. Steel and stone are surfaces, not solid material.

Hold still and you disappear.

The eye responds to movement. Stand still, and after a few seconds it loses interest. It hesitates, searches briefly, then settles. You are still there. Nothing seems to be missing.

A radar sensor detects presence, movement and distance. It recognises no faces and records nothing.

02 / The lens

Artist's note

I forget that I am wearing them.

Every morning I put in my contact lenses. For a moment I feel them. Then, nothing. The world is sharp.

Glasses openly show that someone needs help to see. A contact lens does the same and disappears from view. At some point I no longer want to imagine myself without it.

Feeds work the same way for me. They do not just show me things, they decide what seems important. They suit me. That is exactly why, at some point, I stop asking why I see these particular images.

Even my past runs through this lens. A memories feature shows me selected photos. Others never appear again. Forgetting here does not mean deleted. It means never shown again.

The lens is not bad. It helps. That is exactly why I no longer notice it.

joel van hees

Would I still recognise what the world looked like without the lens?

03 / Adaptation

Who adapts to whom?

The psychologist Jean Piaget describes learning as an exchange between two movements. In assimilation, I fit new things into what I already know. In accommodation, my thinking changes because something does not fit.

A personalised feed shifts this balance. It adapts the world to me instead of me to the world. And I adapt my behaviour to it. My view never has to change.

For Piaget, learning begins with irritation. A system that reliably removes irritation also removes the reasons to learn.

Piaget describes how children develop. Here his terms serve as a tool for thinking, not as proof.

04 / The pull

A change of perspective

THE PULL.

The brightest light surrounds the darkest place.

Artistic simulation

At the centre of the dome lies a hollow. It recalls a pupil and a black hole. Beyond the event horizon, not even light returns. Whatever disappears there sends no word back.

05 / Readings

Nothing is missing.

No guard is needed. The door is open. Every day I choose the world I am offered again, because it is comfortable and it suits me. Whoever can no longer imagine another outside is locked in.

Three images I keep returning to. Readings, not claims about the world.

Plato / The cave

Shadows on the wall.

People see only shadows on the wall and take them for the world. In Plato, they are chained. In my work, the chain is a comfort.

Plato, Republic, Book VII

Maya / A veil

Through something.

In Indian philosophy, Maya describes the veil through which the world appears. I connect the idea with the contact lens: a layer that shapes how I see while remaining almost invisible itself.

Bhagavad Gita / 7.14 ↗

Philip K. Dick / Black Iron Prison

No one notices the order.

Philip K. Dick describes an order so pervasive that no one notices it anymore. For me, this is not an image of threat but one of habit.

Philip K. Dick, VALIS, 1981

The work does not moralise. It shows a state.

AKKOMMODATION, 2026

3D print, lacquer, lens, servo motors, radar sensor, controller

approx. 60 × 60 × 27 cm

Unique piece

The work today

Late build stage, October 2026. The mechanics are running. The lens and the controls are in progress. The digital view uses the current geometry. Here, cursor, scrolling and touch direct the gaze. At the object, a sensor responds to movement.

Tools

The work itself was made with algorithmic tools. I developed print files, geometry and code with Astra 6, Codex and Claude. This adaptation is part of the work too.

In relation

Golan Levin's Opto-Isolator (2007) returns the gaze. AKKOMMODATION asks about the lens in between, and about who disappears.
